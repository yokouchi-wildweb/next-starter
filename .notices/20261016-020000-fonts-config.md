notes: next/font requires loader calls at module top level with literal arguments; importing the variable-name constant from `@/lib/fonts` into the loader call will fail the build — write the string literal. A wrong `variable` name (e.g. shadcn's `"--font-sans"`) cannot be detected by types or at runtime (`.variable` is a hashed class name) and the font would silently not apply; `pnpm test:fonts` statically scans `fonts.config.ts` for `variable: "..."` literals and fails on anything other than the two fixed names — keep it in your test run. `next/font/google` / `local` only work inside the Next compiler: once a loader is added, do not import `fonts.config.ts` from scripts/ or node tests. Google Fonts are fetched at build time and self-hosted (no runtime request); builds in network-restricted CI must allow fonts.googleapis.com / fonts.gstatic.com or use `next/font/local`. Overriding `--font-geist-sans` in `custom/typography.css` takes precedence over a loaded webfont unless you keep `var(--font-sans-loaded, ...)` in your value. Mail templates keep their own inline fontFamily stacks (unaffected).
